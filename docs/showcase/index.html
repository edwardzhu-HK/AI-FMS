01 / THE SYSTEM

Seven movements.
One review workflow.

Years of swimming led me to ask how small movement differences affect control and performance. FMS training gave that question a framework. AI-FMS grew from the practical challenge of reviewing fleeting movement on video.

The complete workflow supports Deep Squat, Hurdle Step, In-Line Lunge, Shoulder Mobility, Active Straight-Leg Raise, Trunk Stability Push-Up, and Rotary Stability.

Upload and define a range; inspect and loop repetitions; examine real MediaPipe pose evidence; accept, correct, or reject an explainable first-pass suggestion; retain the human score and export the review record.

When video quality or protocol information is insufficient, AI can abstain. Pain and clearing conditions remain human decisions.

03 / PHASE I RESEARCH

A full system.
A bounded first study.

The initial quantitative study selected four movements from the seven-movement platform. Its purpose was internal evaluation and exploration of the evidence preserved beneath an ordinal score.

28unique source videos
110canonical repetitions
32formal review items
2 × 2reviewers × blind rounds

Preliminary agreement

The locked AI produced outputs for 28 of 32 items and abstained on four staged-protocol cases. Among 25 comparable items, agreement was 16/25 exact and 23/25 within one point, with mean absolute error 0.44.

Both human review rounds were blind to AI and pose evidence. These results do not measure the benefit of showing AI suggestions.

More than an accuracy number

Deep Squat revealed continuous movement strategies; ASLR preserved bilateral and repeatability differences; Hurdle Step exposed different pathways to the same score; Rotary Stability required a full-cycle event view.

Internal, post-audit evidence from a small convenience corpus. No held-out or clinical validation, injury prediction, or demonstrated improvement in reviewer accuracy.
