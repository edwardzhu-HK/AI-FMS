English film transcript

00:00:01 Hi, I'm Ronnie. Years of swimming taught me to notice how small differences in movement

00:00:05 can affect control, efficiency, and performance.

00:00:11 That curiosity led me to study the Functional Movement Screen, and eventually to build AI-FMS.

00:00:17 FMS stands for Functional Movement Screen.

00:00:21 It is a standardized screening system that uses seven fundamental movement patterns

00:00:26 to observe how mobility, stability, symmetry, and motor control work together.

00:00:32 Each pattern is scored from zero to three.

00:00:34 It is a movement screen, not a medical diagnosis.

00:00:38 Before evaluating other people, I wanted to understand

00:00:41 the protocol responsibly.

00:00:44 I completed FMS Level 1 and Level 2 training and earned both certifications.

00:00:50 The training gave me a structured foundation for setting up the seven screens,

00:00:56 applying the scoring rules, and recognizing when a result requires further human attention.

00:01:01 After the training, I began conducting and reviewing FMS assessments.

00:01:07 I saw four practical limitations.

00:01:09 Movements pass quickly.

00:01:11 Remote review makes repetitions slow to find.

00:01:14 Visual judgment remains qualitative even when angles and trajectories matter,

00:01:19 and the zero-to-three score preserves little of the evidence behind the decision.

00:01:24 Those problems became the starting point for AI-FMS.

00:01:28 AI-FMS is an explainable, human-in-the-loop video-review system.

00:01:33 The workbench supports all seven FMS movements, with repetition-level review

00:01:40 and a movement-specific, pose-based first-pass suggestion.

00:01:42 The AI can explain its evidence and abstain when video quality or protocol information is incomplete.

00:01:48 A reviewer can isolate complete repetitions, replay a difficult moment, and correct the timing

00:01:58 instead of searching through the full video again. The MediaPipe overlay is aligned to the video,

00:02:03 allowing the system to preserve angles, normalized distances, trajectories, and movement events that are hard to record consistently by eye.

00:02:11 The AI suggestion supports, rather than replaces, reviewer judgment.

00:02:16 It shows which criteria support a score, what remains uncertain,

00:02:21 and whether protocol information is missing.

00:02:23 The reviewer records the final score, confidence, camera view, side, clearing information,

00:02:29 and notes.

00:02:30 To evaluate the system without leaking AI information, we built a separate Study Mode.

00:02:36 In both blinded rounds, reviewers could not see AI scores, pose parameters, source file names,

00:02:42 previous answers, or the other reviewer's decisions.

00:02:47 Reviews were stored as append-only events and exported with checksums.

00:02:52 Phase I reconstructed 110 repetitions from 28 source videos

00:02:57 and selected 32 across four movements for two blinded rounds.

00:03:04 In Round B, the reviewers agreed on scoreability for all 32 items and assigned the same score to all 26 jointly scoreable items.

00:03:12 The locked AI exactly matched human consensus on 16 of 25 comparable items

00:03:19 and was within one point on 23. This is an internal benchmark, not held-out clinical validation.

00:03:24 The quantitative evidence also revealed what the ordinal score does not show. Deep Squat formed a strategy continuum;

00:03:30 ASLR preserved side and repeatability differences;

00:03:36 Hurdle Step showed several pathways to the same score;

00:03:42 and Rotary Stability required a full-cycle view of coordination and event sequence.

00:03:50 My role in this project grew far beyond demonstrating a piece of software.

00:03:55 I worked through an AI-assisted development process, but the project taught me that responsible technology

00:04:01 depends on human judgment, data quality, and the willingness to say when the evidence is not enough.

00:04:09 In college, I hope to continue exploring Human Movement Science, biomechanics, and responsible AI.

00:04:15 For me, AI-FMS is not the end of a project.

00:04:19 It is the beginning of a research direction I want to keep pursuing.
